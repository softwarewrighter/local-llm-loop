Have you considered the (very new) Ornith models?  Also, can SmolLLM, or similar small models be configured for speed (MTP and/or speculative decode) and then be fine-tuned to become better coders (distilled from a larger model on a bigger, e.g., 3090-24G, GPU)?

Yes, and I think Ornith is particularly interesting for your use case—but probably not for the reason most people are excited about.

Ornith
The big innovation isn't a new base architecture. It's the RL post-training.

Ornith is trained on top of Gemma 4 and Qwen 3.5 checkpoints, but instead of merely optimizing code generation, it learns its own agent scaffold (planning, tool use, retries, debugging, etc.) during reinforcement learning. In other words, it is optimizing both the coding policy and the orchestration policy simultaneously. 

For someone building an AI software engineering workflow—as we've discussed previously—that is potentially much more valuable than another +2% on a benchmark.

However...

The smallest Ornith today is still 9B

Ornith 397B	large MoE
There isn't (yet) a 3B, 4B, or 2B Ornith.

Given your hardware
Considering your mix of an RTX 5060 (16 GB) and RTX 3090 (24 GB), I'd prioritize a two-stage pipeline:

Teacher: a strong coding model such as a Qwen 3.5/3.6 or Ornith checkpoint on the 3090 generates high-quality coding trajectories, tool calls, and refactorings.

Student: a 0.5B–2B model (SmolLM, Qwen 0.5B, or a similar compact architecture) is LoRA-trained specifically to imitate that teacher's accepted outputs and coding style.

Deployment: use the student as the speculative draft model on your smaller GPUs, paired with the larger verifier.

I suspect that a well-trained 1–2B draft model optimized for your coding workflow could outperform a generic 4B draft model in speculative decoding, because the objective isn't raw intelligence—it's maximizing agreement with the verifier while remaining extremely fast. That's an experiment I'd be very interested in seeing, and it's one that fits well within the hardware you already have.
